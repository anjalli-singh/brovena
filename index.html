Millets: The Everyday Superfood

Old grains with a modern comeback. Easy to cook, rich in fibre and kind to the planet.

Start now

What you need to know

1

Rich in fibre

Whole millets keep you full for longer and are a good swap for polished white rice.

2

Naturally gluten-free

Ragi, jowar, bajra and foxtail are naturally free of gluten, though packaged products should be checked.

3

Climate-friendly

Millets grow in dry conditions with little water, making them a sustainable choice.

🌾

Know your millets

Ragi (finger millet) is high in calcium. Bajra (pearl millet) and jowar (sorghum) make hearty rotis. Foxtail and kodo cook like rice.

The United Nations marked 2023 as the International Year of Millets, which helped bring them back to modern kitchens.

Four easy swaps to begin

1

Mix into atta

Replace about a quarter of your wheat flour with ragi or jowar flour for rotis.

2

Swap the rice

Cook foxtail or kodo millet instead of rice two or three times a week.

3

Try ragi breakfast

Make ragi porridge, dosa or pancakes for a filling start to the day.

4

Soak first

Rinse and soak whole millets for 4 to 6 hours for a softer texture and easier cooking.

Common questions

Are millets good for diabetics?

They generally have a lower glycaemic index than polished rice, but portions still matter. Speak to your doctor or dietitian.

Why soak millets?

Soaking softens the grain, shortens cooking time and can improve digestibility.

How much water do I need?

A common starting point is about 2.5 cups of water per cup of millet, adjusted for the type.
